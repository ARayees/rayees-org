What if the passengers owned the airline?

The concept is a low-cost airline built around membership rather than a traditional loyalty program, combining affordable fares with an annual membership and a community that has a stake in the airline itself.

This model takes that idea and asks a different question: what would the economics actually have to look like for it to work?

Bags, seat selection, priority services, snacks, and other optional purchases can matter almost as much as the advertised fare. In this scenario, the low ticket price is only one part of the passenger relationship.

At the base scenario, each one-percentage-point increase in load factor adds roughly $1.39 million in modeled passenger revenue, making seat occupancy one of the model’s most consequential operating assumptions.

At an 80% investor-to-member conversion rate, raising the annual fee from $45 to $99 adds roughly $482,000 in modeled revenue, with limited upside relative to the potential impact of a higher price on membership demand.
